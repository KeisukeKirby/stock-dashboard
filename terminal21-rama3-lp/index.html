Why barefoot?

Sound familiar?

Most of us have spent decades in shoes that do the work our feet were built to do. These are the four things people tell us most often at the booth.

01

Toes pushed together

A big toe that leans inward, a bump at the joint, toes that overlap. Years inside a pointed toe box leave their mark.

02

Sore soles and heels

Arches that feel flat and tired, heels that ache after a day of standing. Feet that are carried all day rarely get to work.

03

Knees that complain, ankles that wobble

Heavy heel landings travel up the leg, and a thick sole hides the small balance corrections the ankle is meant to make.

04

A stiff lower back

Even a small heel tilts the whole body forward, and the hips and lower back spend the day quietly compensating.

The usual suspect

Three things most shoes do to your feet

01
A narrow, tapered toe box

Your toes are pushed into a point, so they cannot spread to balance you. The foot loses its widest, most stable part.

02
A raised heel

Even one or two centimetres of heel shifts your weight forward. Knees, hips and lower back adjust their angle to keep you upright.

03
A thick, stiff sole

Cushioning mutes what the foot can feel, so the small muscles switch off, and most people land harder than they think.

Barefoot footwear is not a medical treatment. If you have an injury or a diagnosed condition, talk to a professional before changing your shoes, and start slowly (see the four-week guide below).

At the booth

No appointment, no pressure. Come in your usual shoes and leave the rest to us.

01

Every model we have in stock is there to try on. Take a few laps of the floor before you decide anything.

02

Get sized properly

The two brands fit differently, and barefoot sizing is not your usual size. Our staff measure your feet and find the pair that fits.

03

Ask how to start

Going barefoot too fast is how calves get sore. We will tell you how much to wear them in the first weeks.

Your first month

Start slowly

Your feet have been cushioned for years, so the muscles need time to wake up. Here is the pace we suggest at the booth. Everyone is different, so go by how your feet feel.

Week 1
30–60min / day

Walking only, on easy ground: at home, around the mall, a park path. Take them off when your feet feel tired.

Week 2
1–2hours / day

Short errands and commutes on pavement. Pay attention to shorter steps that land under your body rather than out in front.

Week 3
Half days

Most of a normal day on your feet. If you run, add a few minutes of easy jogging at the end of a walk, not a full run yet.

Week 4 and on
Your call

Build up activity gradually: longer walks, then runs or training sessions. Keep your old shoes for the days your feet ask for a break.

!

Calves and the soles of your feet will probably feel worked in the first weeks, the way they do after a new kind of workout. That is the muscles doing their job. Sharp pain is different: if something hurts rather than feels tired, stop for a few days, go back a step, and ask us or a professional.

Good to know

Questions we hear a lot

Anything else, ask the staff at the booth or message us on LINE.

How do I pick the right size?

Barefoot sizing is not your usual sneaker size, and the two brands fit differently. At the booth we measure your foot length and width and let you try the size up and down. If you are shopping from home, use the size charts above: measure your foot standing, from heel to the longest toe, and allow about a thumb-width of room in front.

Do I need five-toe socks for FiveFingers?

You can wear FiveFingers without socks. Many people prefer thin five-toe socks for comfort, sweat and longer days, and we sell them at the booth. Vivobarefoot works with any socks you already own.

How do I wash them, and what about smell?

Most models can be washed by hand or on a gentle cold cycle with mild detergent, then air-dried away from direct sun and heat. Letting them dry fully between wears is the main thing that keeps smell away. Ask the staff about leather models, which need a little more care.

Can I run in them?

Yes, both brands have running models, like the FiveFingers V-Run and the Vivobarefoot Primus Lite IV. The key is to walk in them first and add running gradually over a few weeks, with shorter and lighter steps than you are used to. See the four-week guide above.
